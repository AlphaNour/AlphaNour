A micro-SaaS is two machines

A micro-SaaS is a small online software product, run by one person or a tiny team, that customers pay for every month. Yours has two parts that must both work.

Machine 1

The product machine

The technology. It takes a shipment in and gives answers out: total cost, when to book, whether the quote is fair, whether the deal is safe.

Question it answers: does it work?

Machine 2

The money machine

The business. It turns a stranger who reads your LinkedIn post into a user, and a user into someone who pays every month.

Question it answers: does it pay?

The one idea to remember

The code can be copied. Your data and your rules cannot. That is the business.

Every design choice in this guide protects two things: the real Algeria freight data you collect, and the risk rules that come from your field experience.

Why the engines run on a server, not on the page

On the page (browser)

Anyone can open the page code and copy your formulas, rates and rules in two minutes. Fine for a free lead magnet. Not fine for a paid product.

On a server (API)

Users only see the answers. You update data once and everyone gets it. You can see what people calculate, which tells you what to build next.

How it runs: two flows

The product does two things on repeat: it answers users all day, and it updates itself once a week.

Flow 1 · Every calculation

  1. 1User fills the formRoute, container, value, Incoterm, payment, quote.
  2. 2Screen sends it to the brainOnly the inputs travel, never your formulas.
  3. 3Brain reads the databaseCurrent rate, forecast, duty band, risk rules.
  4. 4Runs the 4 enginesCost, forecast, quote check, risk.
  5. 5Sends answers backThe screen shows them in under a second.
  6. 6Saves the calculationAnonymised. Every use grows your data.

Flow 2 · Every Friday

  1. 1New prices come inYou enter SCFI and WCI in the admin panel, or a script fetches them.
  2. 2Real quotes are addedYour own, and the ones users submitted this week.
  3. 3Algeria premium updatedPer port: Algiers, Oran, Djen Djen…
  4. 4Forecasts recalculated12 weeks ahead, for every route.
  5. 5Alerts sentUsers following a route that moved more than X% get an email.

4

The money machine

How a stranger becomes a paying user, and why each user makes the product better for the next one.

Free (after email)

  • Engine A · landed cost
  • Engine C · quote check
  • Engine D · risk flags

Enough value to use before every shipment. This is what brings people back.

Paid (monthly)

  • Engine B · forecast, Book now / Wait
  • Price alerts on their routes
  • Saved shipments and history
  • PDF report for their boss
  • Team accounts (later)

The 6 numbers to track every week

1Visitorspeople who land on the site
2Signupsgave their email
3Activedid at least one calculation
4Payingsubscribed
5Churn% who cancel each month
6MRRmonthly recurring revenue

If a number drops, the problem is in the step just before it. Few signups means a weak landing page. Few paying users means the paid features aren't valuable enough yet.

Payment: the block to solve first

Stripe doesn't accept Algerian businesses. Options to check:

  • Algerian clients: an Algerian payment gateway like Chargily Pay (Edahabia and CIB cards), or bank transfer with an invoice for B2B.
  • International clients: a reseller service such as Paddle or Lemon Squeezy. Check whether they accept Algerian sellers before planning around them.

This decides how you get paid, so it comes before any Stage 1 code.

Who does what

You need to understand the blocks, not write the code.

You · the moat

  • The data: rates, quotes, duty bands
  • The risk rules from your field experience
  • Selling: LinkedIn, articles, DMs
  • Deciding what's free and what's paid
  • Talking to users every week

Claude · the build

  • Writing all the code
  • Database design
  • The forecast model and backtest
  • Hosting and the weekly robot
  • Fixing and improving as you grow

Your next 7 days

  • Write the rate table for 6 Algerian ports from quotes you already see at work.
  • Write 20 risk rules "If Incoterm X + payment Y, then warn Z." This is your expertise in writing.
  • Start the Friday tracking sheet SCFI, WCI, your Algiers quotes, GRIs announced.
  • Decide the free/paid line Start with the split on page 4.
  • Check payment options What Chargily (or another gateway) needs to open an account.
  • Claude builds Stage 0 the calculator live on your site, with the email gate.

Words you'll hear

FrontendWhat the user sees and clicks (the app screen).
Backend / APIThe server side that does the work and answers the screen (the brain).
DatabaseWhere all the information is stored in tables.
Cron jobA task that runs automatically on a schedule (the Friday robot).
PaywallThe point where free features stop and the user must subscribe.
Free tierThe free plan of a tool like Supabase or Vercel, enough to start.
MRR / ChurnMonthly recurring revenue / % of subscribers who cancel each month.

Tool names and free tiers are current suggestions. Check pricing and country availability before committing.
